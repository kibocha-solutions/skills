# Historical Source Reference

## Procedure

1. Select the source that matches the required doctrine.
2. Open the primary text.
3. Verify the exact section and wording.
4. Extract the legal structure.
5. Do not copy ornamental language without a functional purpose.
6. Keep quotations within applicable limits.
7. Record the source and location.

## Structural patterns

### Magna Carta

- Enumerate legally distinct deprivations.
- State categorical rights.
- Separate judgment authority from procedural delay.

### Petition of Right

- Identify inherited right.
- State the prohibited compulsion.
- Identify the required lawful authority.

### English Bill of Rights

- Name the purported power.
- Declare the power unlawful.
- State the consent or authority requirement.

### Blackstone

- Identify the source of legal authority.
- Pair command and prohibition.
- Define hierarchy before consequence.

### United States Constitution

- State supremacy within the lawful field.
- Bind subordinate decision-makers.
- Use categorical prohibitions.
- Use exact amendment mechanics.

### British North America Act

- Preserve existing law until valid alteration.
- Allocate exclusive legislative fields.
- Distinguish continuation from amendment.

## Modern-source checks

- Use the current official edition.
- Use primary government or intergovernmental publications.
- Verify amendment dates.
- Prefer direct conditions, permissions, obligations, and prohibitions.
- Use Standard vocabulary for plain-language instruments.
- Preserve terms of art whose replacement would change legal effect.
