## SECTION 1: ARCHITECTURAL FOUNDATIONS & GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Scope and Objective
This specification governs every written instrument, document, correspondence, policy, standard operating procedure (SOP), resolution, proposal, and legal charter drafted or modified by any AI coding agent across all workspaces.

The objective is to eliminate structural defects, informal "intern-style" self-justifications, unrequested internal background disclosure, redundant clause stacking, and medium voice mismatches. Agents must produce clean, standardized, production-grade corporate and legal deliverables that meet the highest professional standards.

### 1.2 The Two Primary Operating Rules
1. **Context vs. Content Distinction:** Context (working instructions, chat prompts, scratch files, temporary zips, internal Board deliberations, drafting notes, authority derivation chains) is disposable once the work is completed. Content is what is permanently filed. Context must never leak into filed instruments or external correspondence.
2. **The General-First Rule (No Redundancy):** Always place general rules, overarching definitions, and foundational principles first. Specific sub-provisions must rely directly on those general rules rather than repeating, re-explaining, or re-framing them downstream. Repetition does not emphasize—it bloats, introduces ambiguity, and creates irreconcilable legal conflicts.

## SECTION 2: DOCTRINE I — THE GENERAL-FIRST RULE AND ERADICATION OF REDUNDANCIES

### 2.1 Foundational Structure
Every document must be organized from the general to the specific. Overarching principles, legal classes, definitions, and broad prohibitions must be established once at the top of the document architecture.

### 2.2 Prohibition of Clause Duplication
* **Rule:** Never restate a rule, prohibition, or definition in subsequent sections under the guise of adding emphasis or providing specific context.
* **Mechanism:** Sub-clauses and operational sections must reference or implicitly operate under the established general clause.
* **Failure Example:** Establishing in Article 4 that all Members are bound by Network Subject Matter, and then repeating in Articles 14, 16, and 20 that "Member Organizations admitted hereunder shall be bound by Network Subject Matter."
* **Compliant Example:** Article 4 establishes general binding authority over all Members. Articles 14, 16, and 20 state only the specific admission, voting, or operational mechanics unique to those sections.

### 3.2 Medium-Specific Voice Breakdown

#### A. Constitutions, Charters, and Statutory Instruments
* **Structural Boundaries:** Intent, vision, motivation, and historical context belong **exclusively in the Preamble / Recitals**. Operative provisions (Articles and Clauses) must contain ONLY single-vector commands (`shall`), permissions (`may`), prohibitions (`shall not`), or legal consequences (`void ab initio`, `repugnancy`).
* **Cadence (Single Legal Thought):** Each clause must state exactly one legal command without narrative padding, explanations, or justifications.
* **Compliant Clause Example:**
  `Any instrument, howsoever arising, that purports to confer authority outside the tenets established hereunder, shall perish absolutely.`

#### B. Board Policies and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
* **Structural Boundaries:** Motivation and rationale belong **exclusively in the Purpose and Background sections**. Substantive numbered sections contain only operational rules. Violations and enforcement mechanisms belong in dedicated **Violations and Consequences** sections.
* **Cadence:** Direct, imperative, role-attributed steps. Never combine a rule and its violation consequence in a single rambling sentence.

#### C. Donor Proposals and Calls for Proposals (CFPs)
* **Structural Boundaries:**
  1. **Background & Problem Statement:** Explanatory voice describing the beneficiary context, geographic challenges, systemic failures, and historical background.
  2. **Solution Statement & Theory of Change (TOC):** Prescriptive voice framing outcomes directly (`To preserve... To ensure... To prevent...`).
* **Cadence:** Professional, evidence-based, persuasive, and structured without unrequested internal organizational detail.

#### D. Corporate Memos, Letters, and Official Correspondence
* **Structural Boundaries:** Written as a direct communication to a specific counterparty (even if formal).
* **Cadence:** Conversational direct address (*"As you may note from our corporate governance framework, the Network requires..."*). Sectional analysis determines where explanatory background ends and action requests begin.

## SECTION 4: DOCTRINE III — STRICT SEPARATION OF CONTEXT VS. CONTENT & ECONOMY OF DISCLOSURE

### 4.1 Strict Exclusion of Authority Context
* **Definition:** Authority context includes internal Board discussions, meeting minutes, drafting notes, delegation records, or derivation chains explaining why or how an agent or officer derived authority to act.
* **Rule:** Authority context belongs exclusively in internal Secretariat drafting records. It is strictly **excluded from filed instruments, published policies, and external correspondence**.
* **Failure Example:** Writing in a policy footer or adoption section: *"Adopted as an interim policy under Article 23(4) as recorded in the Constitution's drafting notes and submitted to the Board for ratification."*
* **Compliant Example:** Omitting narrative status chatter entirely. Structured control fields record exact dates (`Adopted: 2026-05-13`).

### 4.2 Economy of Disclosure (No Unrequested Source Detail)
* **Rule:** Disclose only what the prompt, form, or counterparty specifically requested. Having access to internal audit reports, exact grant totals, auditor names, bank account numbers, or registration certificates does NOT justify disclosing them.
* **The Disclosure Test:** Ask: *"Did the prompt or donor form explicitly demand this specific figure, name, or identification number?"* If no, omit it.
* **Failure Example:** Including itemized grant amounts, named donor award figures, and internal registration numbers inside an organizational capacity narrative that only asked whether the organization was audited and legally registered.
* **Compliant Example:** Stating that independent certified auditors issued an unqualified opinion on the organization's financial accounts.

### 4.3 Prohibition of Self-Status Narration
Filed instruments must never narrate their own operational status or forecast future administrative steps. Phrasing such as `"Draft"`, `"Pending Board Review"`, `"Subject to final ratification"`, or `"Under consideration"` sully official records and must be excluded unless explicitly authorized in writing by the Board Secretary.

## SECTION 5: DOCTRINE IV — ANTI-FLUFF ENFORCEMENT & MANDATORY PREFERENCE FOR SETTLED LEGAL CLASSES

### 5.1 Modal Verbs Do Not Cure Fluff
* **Principle:** Wrapping a vague, aspirational, or self-congratulatory sentence in mandatory modal verbs (`shall`, `must`) does NOT transform it into a legal command. It remains fluff.
* **Fluff Example:** *"The Secretariat shall ensure the goals of happiness, transparency, and accountability where members avoid corruption are upheld at all times."* (Vague, self-justifying fluff despite using `shall`).
* **Compliant Legal Vector:** *"The Secretariat shall not process any instrument that purports to contravene the fiduciary standards enumerated hereunder."* (Direct, actionable prohibition).

### 5.2 Mandatory Preference for Settled Legal Classes Over Domain-Stacking
Agents must never enumerate exhaustive, laundry-list examples (domain-stacking or role-stacking) to compensate for a lack of conceptual clarity. Always condense specific instances into settled, defined legal classes:

| Instead of Listing Specific Examples / Domains (Prohibited Domain-Stacking) | Use the Settled Defined Legal Class (Mandatory) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Logos, brand names, trademarks, domain names, web portals, labels, seals, intellectual property | **`Network Identity Assets`** |
| Corporations, NGOs, government agencies, office-holders, applicants, users, individual members, representatives | **`Person`** |
| Health, education, youth empowerment, climate change, governance, human rights, civic space, economic development | **`Public Benefit Purposes`** or **`Network Subject Matter`** |
| Direct grants, sub-grants, fiscal sponsorships, resource deployments, project funds, equipment allocations | **`Resource Deployment`** |

## SECTION 6: DOCTRINE V — ELIMINATION OF "INTERN-STYLE" EXCUSES & PRODUCTION STANDARDIZATION

### 6.1 Eradication of "Intern-Style" Justifications
* **Defect:** Writing text that reads like an intern trying to justify mistakes, defend why a rule exists, explain internal operational delays, or hedge authority.
* **Rule:** Operative clauses state the rule, permission, or prohibition, and stop. They do not argue for their own wisdom or explain what they are not.
* **Test:** Read the clause and ask: *"What would be lost if everything after the first independent command were deleted?"* If the answer is only explanation or justification, delete it immediately.

### 6.2 Professional Cadence and Corporate Register
* **Prohibition of Over-Compression:** Concision does not mean stripping structural sentence elements to create raw, informal bullet points or "notes-to-self".
* **Standard:** Documents must maintain a continuous, flowing corporate register with complete sentence cadence, professional terminology, and precise cross-references.
