# Human Writer

## 1. Establish the brief

1. Identify the audience, purpose, genre, desired voice, length, and requested degree of revision.
2. Preserve the author's meaning, voice, facts, quotations, required structure, and expressly locked wording.
3. Apply documentation, communications, legal, or other applicable skills for the document type.
4. Ask for missing facts only when essential; do not invent evidence, experience, quotations, or citations.
5. Read [research and application](references/storyscope.md) before using the fiction findings.

## 2. Diagnose the writing

1. Identify concrete passages that repeat a point, explain an inference already clear to the reader, use generic phrasing, or disrupt the author's voice.
2. Inspect organization and information flow before changing individual words.
3. For fiction, consider whether theme, causality, character choices, dialogue, time, sensory detail, and resolution serve the story.
4. Treat research patterns as prompts for editorial judgment, not as rules that every story must follow.
5. For factual or instructional writing, retain the explicit explanation, complete steps, and clarity readers need.

## 3. Revise

1. Put the intended point and concrete information where the reader needs them.
2. Remove redundant interpretation, filler, decorative contrast, and stock transitions when they weaken the passage.
3. Choose specific, accurate words and vary sentence rhythm naturally for the writer and audience.
4. Preserve distinctive phrasing and intentional repetition when they serve the work.
5. In fiction, use ambiguity, nonlinear structure, subplots, dialogue, or direct emotion only when the story benefits; do not insert them mechanically.
6. Keep facts, dates, names, numbers, caveats, and citations faithful to the source.
7. Do not promise detector evasion or infer authorship from stylistic features.

## 4. Verify and deliver

1. Read the result as the intended reader and confirm the requested tone, structure, and length.
2. Compare the result against the source for changed claims, lost qualifications, and altered intent.
3. Return the requested writing; include a concise explanation of material changes only when useful or requested.
4. Inspect [examples](examples/revisions.md) for fiction and factual editing patterns.
