# External Correspondence Standards

## 1. Principles of External Correspondence

External correspondence represents the organization to outside stakeholders, regulators, partners, and donors. It must be authoritative, settled, and completely decoupled from internal drafting processes.

## 2. Structural Requirements

1. **Header and Identification**:
   - Institutional letterhead or clear sender identification.
   - Date of dispatch in formal format (e.g., 16 September 2026).
   - Recipient legal name, title, and institutional address.
   - Formal reference line (e.g., `Re: Notice of System Maintenance and Service Continuity`).

2. **Salutation**:
   - Formal address (e.g., `Dear Director [Surname],` or `Dear Members of the Board,`).
   - Avoid casual greetings (`Hi`, `Hello`, `Greetings`).

3. **Operative Narrative**:
   - State the primary purpose in the opening paragraph.
   - Provide necessary context concisely without speculation or hedge words.
   - Group information into logical subsections with descriptive titles when correspondence exceeds one page.
   - Refer to external documents by formal reader-facing title only. Never leak file paths or internal git hashes.

4. **Action and Closing**:
   - Specify required next steps, requested approvals, or submission deadlines clearly.
   - Provide a named contact point for formal replies.
   - Formal sign-off (e.g., `Sincerely,` or `Respectfully submitted,`).
   - Full sender signature block: Name, Official Title, Organization.

## 3. Strict Prohibitions

1. **Zero AI Attribution**:
   - Never include statements such as "Drafted by AI", "Generated by Assistant", or model disclosures.

2. **No Uncertainty Markers**:
   - Do not include provisional tags such as `[DRAFT]`, `[PENDING REVIEW]`, or
     to-be-determined markers.
   - Settle figures before dispatch; do not use quantity hedges ("approximately", "roughly", "about").

3. **No Internal Leakage**:
   - Do not cite internal repository names, branch names, session directories, or internal meeting notes.
