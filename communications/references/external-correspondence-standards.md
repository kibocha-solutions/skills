# External Correspondence Standards

## 1. Principles of External Correspondence

External correspondence represents the organization to outside stakeholders, regulators, partners, and donors. It must be authoritative, settled, and completely decoupled from internal drafting processes, workspace mechanics, and agent deliberation.

## 2. Structural Requirements

1. **Header and Identification**:
   1. Institutional letterhead or clear sender identification.
   2. Date of dispatch in formal format, such as 16 September 2026.
   3. Recipient legal name, title, and institutional address.
   4. Formal reference line, such as `Re: Notice of System Maintenance and Service Continuity`.

2. **Salutation**:
   1. Formal address, such as `Dear Director [Surname],` or `Dear Members of the Board,`.
   2. Avoid casual greetings such as `Hi`, `Hello`, or `Greetings`.

3. **Operative Narrative**:
   1. State the primary purpose in the opening paragraph using the declaratory register (AGENTS.md §9.2): simple present for a stated position or present fact, past tense for a completed event, and `will` for a future event. Do not use command modals (`shall`, `must`).
   2. Provide necessary context concisely without speculation or hedge words.
   3. Group information into logical subsections with descriptive titles when correspondence exceeds one page.
   4. Refer to external documents by formal reader-facing title only. Never leak file paths or internal git hashes.

4. **Action and Closing**:
   1. Specify required recommendations, requested approvals, or submission deadlines clearly.
   2. Never use "Next steps" as a header; use context-appropriate headings such as "Recommendations" or "Action Requested".
   3. Provide a named contact point for formal replies.
   4. Formal sign-off, such as `Sincerely,` or `Respectfully submitted,`.
   5. Full sender signature block: Name, Official Title, Organization.

## 3. Email Correspondence Standards

1. **Subject Line**:
   1. Use Title Case with periods separating distinct clauses, such as `Subject: Project Name. Refined Technical Proposal and Budget`.
   2. Never use Sentence case in subject lines.
   3. Never use a colon `:` to replace an em dash.

2. **Prohibition on Tables**:
   1. Never use Markdown or HTML tables in emails. Email clients render tables inconsistently and frequently break layout on mobile devices.
   2. Format structured data using numbered entries with bold labels or clear inline metrics instead.

3. **List Punctuation and Structure**:
   1. Use a U+2014 em dash `—` specifically at the end of an introductory clause to introduce a list.
   2. Follow Rule C (AGENTS.md §9.11): do not use bullet lists in formal correspondence or attached instruments; use ordered lists with a blank line after every list item; nest with (a), (b), then (i), (ii).
   3. End list items with proper punctuation such as periods or semicolons.

4. **Attachments**:
   1. Never list attachments in a detached block at the bottom of an email.
   2. When reader attention to attachments is required for critical documents such as audit reports or proposals, introduce them in the opening or body text using the standard form:
     "Please find attached a copy of —
     1. Document Title;
     2. Document Title;
     and 3. Document Title."

5. **Exclusion of Personal Identifying Information**:
   1. Never include personal email addresses, national ID, tax, passport, or registration numbers, personal phone numbers, or private individual names unless explicitly authorized by the user.
   2. Exclude unrequested sensitive personal data and internal operational details.

6. **Decoupling from Workspace Mechanics**:
   1. Never include agent thoughts, verification tallies, file system paths, git commands, software locks, or drafting history before, inside, or after the email text. Deliver only the clean email.

## 4. Strict Prohibitions

1. **Zero AI Attribution**:
   1. Never include statements such as "Drafted by AI", "Generated by Assistant", or model disclosures.

2. **No Uncertainty Markers**:
   1. Do not include provisional tags such as `[DRAFT]`, `[PENDING REVIEW]`, or to-be-determined markers.
   2. Settle figures before dispatch; do not use quantity hedges such as "approximately", "roughly", or "about".

3. **No Internal Leakage**:
   1. Do not cite internal repository names, branch names, worktree paths, session directories, or internal meeting notes.

4. **No Classroom/Lecture Headers**:
   1. Do not use "Next steps" as a communication header.
