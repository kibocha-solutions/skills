# Leadership and Incident Formats

## 1. Executive Leadership Updates

Leadership updates must prioritize decisions, risks, blockers, and material outcomes over operational activity.

### Structure

1. **Headline Summary**:
   - One to three sentences outlining overall project or operational health.
   - Clear status classification (Green, Amber, Red).

2. **Decisions Needed**:
   - Specific, actionable choices requiring executive sign-off.
   - Resource or timeline implications for each option.

3. **Key Achievements**:
   - Factual milestones completed during the reporting period.
   - Metric-driven outcomes with exact values.

4. **Active Risks and Mitigations**:
   - Risk description, severity, probability, and responsible owner.
   - Concrete mitigation plan and deadline.

## 2. Incident Communications

Incident updates must be factual, chronological, and calm. Never speculate on root cause while an investigation is ongoing.

### Structure

1. **Current Status**:
   - High-level impact description (e.g., "Partial degradation of European authentication service").
   - Impacted customer cohort and operational scope.

2. **Timeline of Events (UTC)**:
   - Chronological table of verified actions and state transitions.

3. **Active Workstreams**:
   - Engineering containment steps currently in progress.

4. **Next Scheduled Update**:
   - Exact UTC time for the subsequent briefing.
