# Weak Writing Pattern Checks

## Procedure

1. Read the complete artifact as the intended reader.
2. Mark each verified pattern below.
3. Rewrite the complete sentence or paragraph.
4. Remove the underlying rhetorical or structural defect.
5. Re-read the surrounding section.
6. Search for residue after the contextual rewrite.
7. Re-read the complete final artifact.

## Unsupported importance

Remove unsupported significance terms such as:

- testament
- pivotal
- crucial
- vital
- underscores
- broader landscape
- enduring legacy
- key role
- significant shift
- plays a role
- contributes to

State the verified fact, consequence, or unknown.

## Promotional language

Remove or source evaluative language such as:

- boasts
- vibrant
- rich
- profound
- groundbreaking
- renowned
- showcasing
- commitment to
- nestled
- in the heart of
- diverse array

## Unsupported interpretation

Remove trailing interpretation that the source does not establish, including:

- highlighting
- underscoring
- reflecting
- symbolizing
- fostering
- cultivating
- aligning with
- resonating with
- offering valuable insights

## Diminish-to-elevate structure

Remove any construction that elevates one point by diminishing another.

Prohibited forms include:

- not just X, but Y
- not X. Y.
- not just X. This is Y.
- Y, rather than X
- Y, not just X
- not only X, but also Y
- more than X
- went beyond X
- exceeded mere X
- was not limited to X
- not merely X
- X was secondary to Y

Do not preserve the same contrast through synonyms or punctuation. State each relevant fact directly. Keep a contrast only when the distinction itself is the required fact.

## Vague attribution

Replace these forms with named, checkable sources:

- experts argue
- observers note
- industry reports
- critics say
- several sources
- media outlets
- independent coverage
- active social media presence

Do not imply consensus from one or two sources.

## Formulaic structure

Remove:

- speculative future-outlook sections
- conclusions that repeat the body
- inline-header lists when prose or a table is clearer
- decorative emoji
- excessive boldface
- thematic breaks used as decoration
- repeated rule-of-three phrasing
- unnecessary small tables
- title-case headings where the document convention does not require them

## Fourth-wall language

Remove purposeless phrases such as:

- This report analyzes
- This document outlines
- This section discusses
- The purpose of this guide is

Keep only useful cross-references, required structural descriptions, and user-requested discussion of document structure.

## U+2014 punctuation

Remove the U+2014 character from normal prose. Preserve it only where a controlling quotation, formal name, or project-specific title convention requires it. Follow stricter project rules.

## Residue

Remove:

- chatbot openings and offers
- model or knowledge-cutoff disclaimers
- bracketed insertion prompts
- unfinished task markers
- template dates with unknown components
- copied search-result identifiers
- citation-system tokens
- connector attachment tokens
- analytics and model-specific tracking parameters
- malformed Markdown
- mixed markup dialects
- broken links
- unused references
- invalid DOI and ISBN values

## Final checks

- [ ] Every sentence has a specific function.
- [ ] Every evaluative claim is supported or removed.
- [ ] Every attribution names a checkable source.
- [ ] No rhetorical contrast survives under altered wording.
- [ ] No chatbot, placeholder, citation, or tracking residue remains.
- [ ] The final full read passes.

## Drafting-language residue

Check for these patterns that signal incorrect modal register or definition format:

1. **`means` definitions**: Search for the word "means" used to introduce a defined term. Correct form: "A **Term** is ...", never "means".
2. **Bare present-tense legal effects in prescriptive instruments**: Search for phrases such as "does not apply", "is void", "has no legal effect", "remains in effect" in policies, codes, frameworks, standards, procedures, SOPs, manuals, protocols, and guidelines. The correct prescriptive modal is `will`: "will not apply", "will be void", "will have no legal effect", "will remain in effect".
3. **`shall` in prescriptive instruments**: Search for `shall` in policies, procedures, SOPs, manuals, protocols, and guidelines. Prescriptive instruments use `must` for obligations and `will` for declared effects. Reserve `shall` for legislative instruments.
4. **Bullet lists in instruments**: Search for unordered (bullet) lists in legislative, prescriptive, or declaratory instruments. Replace with ordered lists separated by blank lines.
5. **Duties assigned to things**: Search for constructions where a document, agreement, system, policy, or other inanimate subject is given a duty. Reattribute to the responsible position.
6. **Argument in Background or recitals**: Check that Purpose, Background, and recital sections state only the occasion and controlling facts, without argument, explanation, or deliberation.
