# NGO and Donor Narrative

## Procedure

1. Read the donor instructions, call, template, and evaluation criteria in full.
2. Identify the applicant voice, audience, required sections, limits, and evidence rules.
3. Use first-person plural and forward-looking language.
4. Write narrative sections as paragraphs and tables.
5. Keep single-phrase bullet and numbered lists out of proposal narrative.
6. Put need and context in Background or Needs.
7. Put outcomes in Objectives, Approach, Solution, Results, or the donor's named section.
8. Integrate statistics into the surrounding condition.
9. Name the source body and date in the sentence.
10. Add a bibliography only when the donor requires one.
11. Describe community evidence without inventing a person, quotation, or case.
12. Show community participation in needs research and implementation when supported by the sources.
13. Use section headings that name the section.
14. Apply the donor template.
15. Follow the applicable project-specific formatting reference when no
    controlling template exists.
16. Read the complete final narrative as the donor.
17. Verify every claim, figure, date, location, organization, and citation.
18. Remove internal paths, production notes, compliance narration, approval
    status, uncertainty markers, and unsupported claims.

## Attribution

- Write: In 2025, the named source recorded the stated finding.
- Do not append report-style parenthetical citations to call narrative unless the donor requires that citation style.
- Keep full reference details in a bibliography only when required.
- Keep URLs out of prose unless the donor requires them there.

## Tone

- Describe the systemic condition.
- Connect each statistic to the condition it measures.
- Use evidence-backed community voice.
- Do not invent a beneficiary identity, history, event, or quotation.
- Do not use promotional headings.
- Do not dramatize evidence.
- Do not present bare statistics without context.

Use headings such as Background, Organisation Overview, Objectives, Theory of
Change, Logic Frame, Work Plan, Budget, Monitoring and Evaluation, and
Sustainability when those sections are required.
